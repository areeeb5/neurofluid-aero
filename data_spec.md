# NeuroFluid-Aero — Data Spec

Shared contract for every stage of the data pipeline (CST airfoil geometry
-> XFOIL -> SU2 [optional] -> ANSYS -> 64x64 grid encoding). This is the
one place tensor shapes, file formats, and non-dimensionalization get
defined — update it here, not ad hoc in individual scripts, whenever a
stage's output format changes or a new stage is added.

## Conventions used everywhere
- Non-dimensional, chord-normalized coordinates: chord length `c = 1.0`,
  leading edge at `x = 0`, trailing edge at `x = 1`. All `(x, y)` are in
  units of chord fraction (dimensionless).
- Airfoil-fixed coordinate frame: `x` along the chord line, `y`
  perpendicular to it. Angle of attack is applied downstream (e.g. by
  XFOIL, or by rotating the grid), never baked into the stored geometry.
- Every data-generation script takes `--seed` and is fully reproducible.

## Phase 1, Step 1 — airfoil geometry (perturbed real baselines)
Generated by [`data_generation/cst_airfoil.py`](data_generation/cst_airfoil.py),
[`data_generation/cst_fit.py`](data_generation/cst_fit.py) and
[`data_generation/generate_airfoils.py`](data_generation/generate_airfoils.py).

### Why this method (revised — the first approach was replaced)
The original version sampled CST coefficients independently from wide
fixed ranges. Nothing in that scheme guaranteed the result was
*aerodynamically* well-behaved — the validity filter only checked
self-intersection and a thickness band, both of which a bad airfoil can
pass. Concretely: the first Bernstein coefficient of each surface sets
the **leading-edge radius**, and sampling it near zero yields a cusped,
zero-radius nose. 40% of that dataset had at least one LE coefficient
clipped to exactly 0.0, and XFOIL's viscous solver could not resolve
them — those cases failed even at α = 0, not merely near stall.

Measured on an identical 20-airfoil × 15-α pilot:

| | old (independent CST sampling) | new (perturbed baselines) |
|---|---|---|
| converged | 48.7% | **96.0%** |
| failed | 37.7% | 4.0% |
| timed out | 13.7% | **0.0%** |
| mean time / airfoil sweep | 28.6 s | **3.8 s** |

Each airfoil is now a small **multiplicative** perturbation of CST
coefficients fitted to a real airfoil. Multiplicative noise preserves
each coefficient's sign and magnitude, so a healthy leading-edge radius
is inherited from the real baseline *by construction* rather than hoped
for and filtered afterwards.

### Baselines (`data_generation/baselines/`)
15 real airfoils from the UIUC Airfoil Coordinate Database
(<https://m-selig.ae.illinois.edu/ads/coord_database.html>), downloaded by
[`download_baselines.py`](data_generation/download_baselines.py) and
**committed** — they are small text inputs and pinning them keeps
generation reproducible if upstream changes.

`naca0006, naca0012, naca0018, naca2412, naca4412, naca6409, naca23012,
clarky, e387, s1223, goe387, naca4415, naca63-412, rae2822, sd7062`

Three use different UIUC filenames (same airfoils): `naca0012`→`n0012`,
`naca6409`→`n6409`, `naca63-412`→`n63412`.

Files arrive in two formats and both are parsed: **Selig** (one loop, TE →
LE → TE) and **Lednicer** (a point-count line, then upper LE → TE, then
lower LE → TE).

### Baseline normalization (required before fitting)
UIUC files are not consistently normalized — sd7062's leading edge sits
at (0.00027, −0.00327), e387's at (0.00044, +0.00234). This matters
because the CST class function pins `y(0) = 0`, so fitting an
un-normalized airfoil forces an irreducible error at the nose. For
sd7062 it distorted the fit enough that the upper surface crossed below
the lower one near the LE and **100% of its perturbations were rejected**.

`cst_fit.normalize_surfaces` therefore translates the LE to (0,0),
rotates the chord line horizontal, and scales the chord to 1 before
fitting. After this every baseline has LE y = 0 exactly.

### CST fitting (linear least-squares)
With the class function and Bernstein basis fixed, a CST surface is
**linear** in its coefficients, so fitting uses `numpy.linalg.lstsq` — no
nonlinear optimizer:

    y(x) = C(x) * sum_i A_i K_i(x)  +  x * te_offset

The design matrix is built as `C(x_j) * K_i(x_j)` rather than dividing
the data by `C(x)`; `C(x)` vanishes at both x=0 and x=1, so dividing
would blow up exactly at the leading and trailing edge. `te_offset` is
pinned to each surface's own y at x=1, so the fit reproduces the real
trailing edge exactly.

Fit quality (5 coefficients/surface, max deviation in chord fractions):
13 of 15 baselines are within 0.0013 c away from the immediate nose
(x > 0.005). Residual error is concentrated in the first ~0.1% of chord,
where CST's fixed `N1 = 0.5` exponent cannot match the discrete nose
points — this is a known property of the parameterization, not a bad
fit. `clarky` (flat lower surface), `s1223` (extreme high-lift) and
`goe387` (only 17 points/surface) carry slightly larger genuine
residuals. All are far below the intentional 10% perturbation.

**Coefficient count is 5 per surface, chosen empirically.** 8
coefficients fit the geometry better but converged *worse* in XFOIL
(93.6% vs 95.1%, with timeouts): extra degrees of freedom give the
perturbation more room to make the surface wavy, which costs more than
the improved fit gains.

### `data_generation/airfoils/<airfoil_id>.dat`
Selig-format coordinate file, plain text, one airfoil per file.
- Line 1: airfoil name/ID (e.g. `airfoil_0001`)
- Remaining lines: `x y`, whitespace-separated, 6 decimal places
- Point order: upper-surface trailing edge -> leading edge -> lower-surface
  trailing edge (the leading-edge point appears once, shared between the
  two surfaces). This is the format XFOIL expects via `LOAD <file>`
  (Step 4 of the pipeline).
- `x` in `[0, 1]`; `y` typically in `[-0.15, 0.15]` (chord fractions)

### `data_generation/airfoil_index.csv`
One row per airfoil, master index joining ID -> CST parameters -> derived
geometry stats.

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `airfoil_id` | unique ID, e.g. `airfoil_0001` |
| `filename` | corresponding `.dat` filename in `airfoils/` |
| `baseline` | real airfoil this variant was perturbed from, e.g. `naca4412` |
| `variant_index` | 0-based index of this variant within its baseline |
| `upper_0` … `upper_4` | 5 CST (Bernstein) coefficients, upper surface |
| `lower_0` … `lower_4` | 5 CST (Bernstein) coefficients, lower surface |
| `te_upper`, `te_lower` | per-surface trailing-edge y offset inherited from the fitted baseline (real airfoils rarely have a perfectly symmetric TE gap, so upper and lower each carry their own instead of sharing ±half of one thickness) |
| `max_thickness` | `max(y_upper - y_lower)` over the chord, chord fraction |
| `max_camber` | signed camber-line value at the point of max `\|camber\|`, chord fraction (sign flips depending on whether the airfoil is cambered up or down there — this can make thickness/camber scatter plots look bimodal near camber=0, that's expected, not a bug) |

### CST parameterization
- Class function: `C(x) = x^0.5 * (1-x)^1` (standard round-nose/sharp-tail
  subsonic template)
- Shape function: Bernstein polynomial, 5 coefficients per surface,
  `S(x) = sum_{i=0}^{4} A_i * C(4,i) * x^i * (1-x)^(4-i)`
- Surface: `y(x) = C(x) * S(x) + x * te_half_thickness`
- x-distribution: cosine-spaced (bunched near LE/TE), 100 points per
  surface by default (`--n_points`)
- Trailing-edge thickness: small, configurable (`--te_thickness`,
  default `0.002` = 0.2% chord, split symmetrically ±half between upper
  and lower); `0` gives a sharp TE

### Validity filter (applied to every airfoil before it's accepted)
- Rejected if the upper surface is below the lower surface anywhere
  (self-intersecting)
- Rejected if max thickness < 2% chord or > 22% chord
- Filter is unit-tested (`generate_airfoils.py::run_validity_tests`)
  against a deliberately crossing case, a too-thin case, and a too-thick
  case (all must be rejected), plus one normal case (must be accepted),
  before any real data is generated.

### Perturbation / sampling
- Each variant's coefficients are `fitted_coeff * (1 + eps)` with
  `eps ~ Normal(0, 0.10)` drawn independently per coefficient
  (`--perturb`, default 0.10 = 10%).
- Multiplicative, not additive: this preserves each coefficient's sign
  and magnitude, which is what keeps the leading-edge radius healthy and
  the variant recognizably "close" to its baseline.
- `te_upper` / `te_lower` are inherited unperturbed from the fitted
  baseline.
- 500 airfoils split as evenly as possible across the 15 baselines
  (34 each for the first 5, 33 for the rest).
- Seeded by `--seed` (default 42) via `numpy.random.default_rng`;
  verified reproducible (0/500 coordinate files differ on a re-run).
- Generation aborts if any baseline exceeds `n_want * --max_attempt_factor`
  rejections, so a pathological baseline fails loudly instead of looping
  forever.

### Acceptance rate — and why it is the wrong health metric
The new run accepts **500/500 (100%)**. The *old* approach also accepted
500/500 (100%). The filter rejected nothing in either case, which is
exactly the point: **acceptance rate never detected the defect.** The
degenerate zero-radius noses passed the filter cleanly and only showed
up as XFOIL non-convergence two steps later. Judge geometry health by
the XFOIL convergence rate (table above), not by this number.

## Phase 1, Step 3 — XFOIL install + validation

### Install
XFOIL 6.99 (Mark Drela / Harold Youngren, MIT), downloaded from
<https://web.mit.edu/drela/Public/web/xfoil/> as `XFOIL6.99.zip`
(3,813,300 bytes, SHA-256
`E13E8FE5CC38D8AC2626E9D3B17643BDCFAA63791619F042AFDAA7CD103BCB08`).
Extracted to `tools/xfoil/` — **`tools/` is gitignored**; it's a
third-party binary and does not belong in repo history. The single zip
contains all three executables (`xfoil.exe`, `pplot.exe`, `pxplot.exe`)
plus `Xfoil699src.zip`; the separately-listed `Pplot.zip`/`Pxplot.zip` on
the download page are NOT needed.

### How XFOIL is driven (the mechanism Step 4's batch runner uses)
XFOIL is a text-menu program that reads commands from **stdin**, one
command per line, so it's fully scriptable by feeding it a command file.
Blank lines mean "go up one menu level".

Reliability notes learned here, which Step 4 must follow:
- Redirect stdin/stdout to **files** (e.g. PowerShell `Start-Process
  -RedirectStandardInput/-RedirectStandardOutput`). Piping XFOIL's stdout
  into another process (`xfoil.exe < cmds.txt | head`) **hangs**.
- Start every script with `PLOP` / `G` / blank line to toggle graphics
  OFF, or XFOIL will try to open plot windows during a headless run.
- Run with the working directory set to the output folder and use short
  relative filenames; XFOIL has a limited filename buffer.
- Always terminate the command file with `QUIT`, and use a wall-clock
  timeout — a malformed script leaves XFOIL blocked on a prompt forever.
- `MRCHDU: Convergence failed ...` in the log is a benign *inner*
  boundary-layer iteration warning, NOT a failed point. The real check is
  that a point reached the polar: count `Point added to stored polar`
  lines, and treat `VISCAL` failures as genuine non-convergence.

### Validation case (`data_generation/xfoil_validation/`)
Per the "validate against a known published case first" convention, XFOIL
was checked against **NACA 0012** (generated natively via `NACA 0012`, no
coordinate file) at **Re = 1e6, M = 0.15, Ncrit = 9 (default), free
transition**, α from −4° to +10° in 1° steps. All 15 points converged.

| file | contents |
|---|---|
| `run_polar.inp`, `run_cp.inp` | exact XFOIL command scripts used (the reproducible recipe) |
| `run_polar.log`, `run_cp.log` | full solver output, kept as convergence evidence |
| `naca0012_polar.txt` | `PACC` polar: `alpha CL CD CDp CM Top_Xtr Bot_Xtr` (12 header lines) |
| `cp_alpha0.txt`, `cp_alpha4.txt` | `CPWR` surface Cp: `x y Cp` (3 header lines), Selig node order (TE → LE → TE) |
| `naca0012_validation.png` | Cp at 0°/4° + lift curve |

`Top_Xtr` / `Bot_Xtr` are the free-transition x/c locations on the upper
and lower surface — these are the transition columns `CLAUDE.md` wants
retained for the laminar-turbulent stretch goal.

### Validation checks (`data_generation/validate_xfoil.py`)
NACA 0012 is symmetric, so correctness is checkable a priori. The script
re-runs these and exits nonzero on failure:

| check | tolerance | result |
|---|---|---|
| `CL(α=0) == 0` | 1e-4 | 0.000000 |
| `CM(α=0) == 0` | 1e-3 | −0.000000 |
| Cp upper == Cp lower at α=0 | 5e-3 | max diff 3.97e-4 |
| `CL(−α) == −CL(α)` | 5e-4 | 0.0 |
| `CD(−α) == CD(α)` | 5e-4 | 0.0 |
| `Top_Xtr(α) == Bot_Xtr(−α)` | 1e-3 | 2.0e-4 |
| lift-curve slope ≈ 2π/rad (0.1097/deg) | ±0.01 | 0.1083 /deg |

Rerun with:
```
python data_generation/validate_xfoil.py
```

## Phase 1, Step 4 — batch XFOIL over all 500 airfoils
Produced by [`data_generation/run_xfoil_batch.py`](data_generation/run_xfoil_batch.py),
reported by [`data_generation/report_xfoil_batch.py`](data_generation/report_xfoil_batch.py).

### Run configuration
500 airfoils × 15 angles of attack (−4° to +10°, 1° steps) = 7,500 cases,
at **Re = 1e6, M = 0.15, Ncrit = 9 (default), free transition** — the same
flow conditions the Step 3 NACA 0012 validation used.

`PPAR N 200` fixes the panel count at **200 nodes for every airfoil**, so
Cp arrays are a consistent shape across the dataset and match CLAUDE.md's
"~200 surface points" for Head A. The x/y node positions are identical
across all angles of one airfoil (verified: deviation exactly 0.0), so
they are stored once per airfoil rather than per case.

### Execution model
One XFOIL process handles one airfoil's **entire** angle sweep: the
boundary layer marches from the previous angle, which is both faster and
better-converging than restarting each angle. Consequences:

- The wall-clock timeout is therefore **per airfoil sweep**, not per
  angle. It is sized from measured per-*angle* timing: median 0.123 s,
  p99 3.20 s, slowest single angle observed **3.75 s**. A pathological
  sweep with every angle that slow is ~56 s, so the default **240 s** is
  ~4.3× margin on that and ~19× the slowest sweep actually observed.
- Parallelism is across airfoils, default `cpu_count - 2` workers, each
  in its **own working directory** (`_work/<airfoil_id>/`), so concurrent
  XFOIL processes cannot collide over scratch filenames.

Follows the Step 3 reliability rules: stdin/stdout redirected to real
files (never pipes), every script starts `PLOP` / `G` / blank, and
convergence truth is whether a point reached the stored polar — *not* the
absence of `MRCHDU` warnings.

### `data_generation/xfoil_data/status.csv`
The complete ledger — one row per **(airfoil, angle)** pair, 7,500 rows.
Nothing is ever silently dropped. Also the resume ledger: on startup any
airfoil with a full set of recorded angles is skipped.

| column | meaning |
|---|---|
| `airfoil_id` | e.g. `airfoil_0001` |
| `baseline` | real baseline family this airfoil came from |
| `alpha` | angle of attack, degrees |
| `status` | `converged`, `failed`, or `timed_out` |

### `data_generation/xfoil_data/polars.csv`
Aggregated aerodynamic coefficients, one row per **converged** case.
Rebuilt from the `.npz` files at the end of every run rather than
appended during it, so a resumed run cannot duplicate rows.

Columns: `airfoil_id, baseline, alpha, CL, CD, CDp, CM, Top_Xtr, Bot_Xtr`

`Top_Xtr` / `Bot_Xtr` are free-transition x/c locations on the upper and
lower surface — the transition columns CLAUDE.md wants kept for the
laminar-turbulent stretch goal.

### `data_generation/xfoil_data/cp/<airfoil_id>.npz`
One compressed archive per airfoil, holding only its converged angles.

| key | shape | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `alpha` | (n_conv,) | converged angles, degrees |
| `x`, `y` | (200,) | panel node coordinates, shared by all angles |
| `cp` | (n_conv, 200) | surface pressure coefficient |
| `CL`, `CD`, `CDp`, `CM`, `Top_Xtr`, `Bot_Xtr` | (n_conv,) | per-angle coefficients |
| `baseline` | scalar string | real baseline family |

Node order is Selig (TE → LE → TE), matching the `.dat` files and the
Step 3 `CPWR` output.

### Results of the full run
7,500 cases in **9.3 min** on 6 workers: **94.33% converged**, 5.43%
failed, 0.24% timed out. Output is 10.8 MB. Full report in
[`data_generation/xfoil_batch_report/`](data_generation/xfoil_batch_report/).

A few percent of scattered non-convergence is **normal XFOIL behaviour,
not a defect to chase**. But the per-baseline breakdown — which the
20-airfoil pilot was far too small to reveal — shows the failures are
*not* uniformly distributed:

| baseline | converged | note |
|---|---|---|
| naca23012 … naca0018 (12 families) | 95.1 – 99.0% | sparse, scattered |
| goe387 | 89.5% | thin + cambered |
| naca6409 | 88.5% | cluster near α ≈ 2° |
| **naca0006** | **72.5%** | the only family with timeouts |

**naca0006 is a physics result, not a bug.** Its failures are clean
through α = 4° and then climb monotonically — 6, 10, 15, 23, 29, 30
failures at α = 5…10°, i.e. every variant fails at α = 10°. A 6%-thick
section has a very small leading-edge radius and separates abruptly from
the LE at Re = 1e6 with no reattachment, so there is no steady attached
solution for XFOIL to converge to. Real NACA 0006 stalls around 8–9°.
All 18 timeouts are naca0006 variants grinding at those post-stall
angles.

**Consequence for training:** high-α coverage is systematically thinner
for thin sections. This is a property of the data, not an error, but any
model evaluation should not assume uniform α coverage across baselines.

One airfoil (`airfoil_0192`, a naca6409 variant) failed at all 15 angles
and therefore has **no `.npz` file**. Its 15 failures are still recorded
explicitly in `status.csv`; consumers must not assume every `airfoil_id`
in the index has a corresponding Cp archive.

## Phase 1, grid encoding — the 64x64 grid (THE shared grid definition)
Per `CLAUDE.md`, all three data tiers (XFOIL / SU2 / ANSYS) must land on
the SAME grid and the same non-dimensionalization. This section is that
definition; the code constants in
[`data_generation/grid_encoding.py`](data_generation/grid_encoding.py) must
match it. **ANSYS fields must be exported on exactly this grid.**

Chosen 2026-10-11 (Areeb, "Tight" option). Decided and implemented in the
overnight run; the ANSYS Meshing session has NOT yet been told.

### Domain and indexing
| item | value |
|---|---|
| frame | airfoil-fixed, chord units (c = 1, LE at origin, TE at x = 1) |
| x range | **−0.25 to 1.25** |
| y range | **−0.75 to 0.75** |
| resolution | 64 × 64 cells |
| cell size | 0.0234375 c in x and y (square cells), about 43 cells per chord |
| sampling points | **cell centres**: `x_i = −0.25 + (i + 0.5)·dx`, same for y |
| array layout | `[iy, ix]`, `iy` increases with y (y up), `ix` increases with x |

Cell-centre sampling means torch `grid_sample(..., align_corners=False)` maps
physical (x, y) to [−1, 1] by `u = (x + 0.25)/1.5·2 − 1`,
`v = (y + 0.75)/1.5·2 − 1` (`grid_encoding.xy_to_norm`).

### Channels (model input, order fixed)
| # | name | definition |
|---|---|---|
| 0 | `sdf` | signed distance to the airfoil outline, chord units, **negative inside, positive outside** |
| 1 | `mask` | 1 inside the airfoil, 0 outside; exactly `(sdf < 0)` |
| 2 | `x` | physical x of the cell centre (chord units) |
| 3 | `y` | physical y of the cell centre (chord units) |
| 4 | `alpha` | angle of attack in degrees **÷ 10**, constant over the grid |
| 5 | `re` | **log10(Re) ÷ 6**, constant over the grid (Re = 1e6 gives 1.0) |

Channels 0–3 depend only on the airfoil, so they are stored once per airfoil.
Channels 4–5 are scalars added at load time
(`grid_encoding.assemble_input`). Angle of attack is never baked into the
geometry.

### `data_generation/grids/airfoil_grids.npz` (gitignored, regenerated in ~30 s)
| key | shape | meaning |
|---|---|---|
| `airfoil_id` | (500,) | same order as `airfoil_index.csv` |
| `sdf` | (500, 64, 64) float32 | channel 0 |
| `mask` | (500, 64, 64) uint8 | channel 1 |
| `x`, `y` | (64, 64) float32 | channels 2, 3 (identical for every airfoil) |
| `domain` | (4,) | `[x_min, x_max, y_min, y_max]` |

Rebuild: `python data_generation/grid_encoding.py`. The SDF is computed
from the `.dat` outline (exact distance to the closed polygon, including the
trailing-edge gap), not from the 200 XFOIL panel nodes.

### Validation (`data_generation/test_grid_encoding.py`, all pass)
Known-good: exact circle SDF matches `r − R` to 2.5e-7; symmetric airfoil
gives an exactly symmetric SDF and mask; SDF is 0 on the outline; mask area
within 3% of the true polygon area (0.14%, 0.71%, 2.43% on three airfoils);
`|∇SDF| = 1` away from the surface (99th percentile deviation 0.010).
Known-bad: self-intersecting, 2-point, zero-area and NaN outlines are all
rejected.

Over all 500 airfoils (`check_grids.py`, `grids/grid_stats.txt`): no empty
masks; the 200 XFOIL surface nodes lie within **0.52 of a cell** of the SDF
zero level set (mean 0.043 cell), so Head A can sample the latent field at
those nodes.

### Known limitation: thin sections are coarse in the mask
At 43 cells per chord a thin wing is only a few cells thick: the thickest
column holds a median of 5 mask cells (range 2 to 8), and **34 airfoils (the
6%-thick NACA 0006 family) have a thickest column of only 2 cells**. For
those the mask is coarse and stops short of the thin trailing edge; the SDF
channel still carries the sub-cell geometry, and Head A samples the SDF-aligned
latent field. Expect thin-section accuracy to be the weak spot. See
`grids/grid_check.png`.
